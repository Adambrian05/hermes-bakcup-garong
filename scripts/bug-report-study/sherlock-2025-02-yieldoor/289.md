# Only checking `isLiquidateable()` during position opening may be vulnerable to MEV attacks.

### Summary

Although `isLiquidateable()` can prevent immediate liquidation, it may still be vulnerable to MEV and price manipulation attacks.  
MEV bots can front-run the execution of `isLiquidateable()` and liquidate positions, allowing attackers to profit.

### Root Cause

https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L195-L199

* **Static Check During Position Opening**  
The `isLiquidateable()` check during position opening is only a static check.  

However, if the price drops instantaneously, the position may still immediately become eligible for liquidation!  
If an attacker borrows funds and dumps the tokens, the protocol may fail to react in time, leading to potential issues.  

* **Lack of Dual Verification for TWAP Price**  
The `isLiquidateable()` function may rely on the spot price, which attackers can manipulate.  
If the spot price is used for checks, it may not reflect the true market value in the short term, allowing attackers to exploit high leverage for arbitrage.  

* **Liquidation May Be Front-Run by MEV Bots**  
MEV bots can front-run liquidations, executing transactions ahead of normal users, enabling attackers to profit while the protocol incurs losses.

### Attack Path

* **Price Manipulation**  
The attacker uses flash loans or self-trading to temporarily inflate the price of token0 or token1, making them appear more valuable.  

* **Borrowing at Inflated Prices**  
Due to the temporarily inflated prices, the attacker can borrow funds exceeding their actual value and deposit them into the Vault to open leveraged positions.  

* **Price Crash**  
After completing the borrowing, the attacker immediately dumps the tokens, restoring the price to its true level or even causing a further price drop through large sell orders.  

* **Profit from Liquidation**  
As the position is now in a loss, the contract automatically triggers the liquidation mechanism.  
The attacker, or an MEV bot, profits by liquidating the position and earning liquidation rewards.

### Impact

* MEV bots front-run liquidations, undermining the long-term stability of the protocol.  
* Reduced liquidity ultimately leads to the collapse of the protocol.  
* Healthy positions being incorrectly liquidated erode user trust.

no

### Mitigation

* Implement a mandatory waiting mechanism that prohibits users from liquidating newly opened positions within a short time frame.  
* Raise the liquidation threshold and add additional conditions for triggering liquidation to avoid frequent liquidations.  
* Use a dual verification system combining TWAP and spot prices to mitigate the impact of sudden price fluctuations on positions.
* Implement anti-MEV mechanisms to prevent bots from front-running liquidations and causing losses to the protocol.
