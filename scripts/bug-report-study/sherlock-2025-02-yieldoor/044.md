# Incomplete Contract Pause Mechanism Leads to Liquidity Risk

### Summary

When the contract is paused (`paused = true`), functions such as `repay`, `pushFunds`, and `pullFunds` remain operational, allowing a `leverager` to withdraw assets from the system. This undermines the purpose of the pause mechanism, making the system vulnerable during emergencies.

### Root Cause

The `LendingPool` contract uses the paused variable to control operational status. When `paused = true`, functions like `deposit`, `redeem`, and `borrow` are blocked using the `notPaused` modifier. However, `repay`, `pushFunds`, and `pullFunds` do not implement `notPaused`, allowing a `leverage`r to continue withdrawing assets (`pullFunds`) or adjusting positions (`pushFunds`) even when the system is paused. This creates a liquidity risk and defeats the purpose of the pause mechanism.

### Internal Pre-conditions

- The `leverager` is authorized to call `pullFunds`, `pushFunds`, and `repay`.
- The contract is paused (`paused = true`).

### External Pre-conditions

The `leverager` has assets in the `LendingPool`.
The `leverager` has sufficient balance to execute `pullFunds` or `repay`.

### Attack Path

1. The owner triggers `emergencyPauseAll()` to pause the system.
2. The `leverager` calls `pullFunds()` to withdraw assets from `LendingPool` (not blocked due to missing notPaused).
3. Assets are withdrawn, reducing system liquidity.
4. Other users are unable to redeem due to insufficient liquidity, leading to loss of trust.

### Impact

The leverager can drain assets, preventing the system from fulfilling users redemption requests.
