# Incorrect Fee Collection Due to Misreferenced Tick Boundary

### Summary

The `Strategy.sol::collectFees` function incorrectly references `mainPosition.tickUpper` instead of `vestPosition.tickUpper` when collecting fees for an ongoing vesting position. This mistake results in an incorrect fee collection range, potentially leading to miscalculated or lost fees. As a result, the protocol may fail to collect the correct amount of fees from vested positions, impacting expected revenue distribution.

### Root Cause

The root cause of the issue is the incorrect reference to `mainPosition.tickUpper` instead of `vestPosition.tickUpper` when calling `collectPositionFees` for the vesting position. This mistake leads to collecting fees from the wrong tick range, which can result in inaccurate fee distribution.

```solidity
collectPositionFees(vestPosition.tickLower, mainPosition.tickUpper); // @audit-issue should be vestPosition.tickUpper instead of mainPosition.tickUpper
```

### Attack Path

1. Fee Collection Mistake: The function incorrectly collects fees from `mainPosition.tickUpper` instead of the actual vested position range.
2. Protocol Fee Miscalculation: Since the protocol fee is calculated based on the incorrectly collected fees, the protocol either over-collects or under-collects its expected share.
3. Potential Fund Losses:
If too many fees are collected, liquidity providers (LPs) lose earnings, discouraging participation.
If too few fees are collected, the protocol loses revenue, which could impact its ability to sustain operations.
4. Exploitable Arbitrage: A malicious user might front-run fee collection transactions and manipulate the fee calculation to withdraw an unfair share.

### Impact

The incorrect tick range used in fee collection results in misallocated or uncollected fees. This can lead to:

1. Loss of Vested Fees: Fees from the vesting position may not be properly collected, causing financial losses for users entitled to them.
2. Incorrect Fee Distribution: Fees may be incorrectly attributed to other positions, leading to miscalculations in rewards or protocol earnings.
3. Accounting Inconsistencies: The protocol's fee tracking mechanism may record incorrect values, causing potential discrepancies in financial reporting.
4. Potential Exploitation: If an attacker identifies this issue, they could manipulate positions to maximize their fee collection at the expense of other users.
