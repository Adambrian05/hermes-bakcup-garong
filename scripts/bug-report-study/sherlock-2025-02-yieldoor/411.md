# Unintended Slippage

### Summary

The Vault’s withdrawal process is designed to redeem Vault shares for underlying tokens in proportion to the total assets held in the Strategy. Under normal conditions, idle (i.e. non-deployed) balances cover the requested withdrawal amounts. However, if the idle balances are insufficient, the Vault calls the Strategy’s `withdrawPartial` function—which forcibly removes liquidity from both the main and secondary positions to cover the shortfall.

This forced liquidity removal may occur at unfavorable pool prices and incur significant slippage. As a result, users can receive substantially fewer tokens than calculated based solely on the share ratio. This discrepancy violates user expectations and can lead to financial losses, undermining trust in the protocol.


### Root Cause

Fallback to WithdrawPartial:
The Vault’s withdraw function calculates the desired amounts of token0 and token1 by applying a proportional share of the total Strategy balances. It then checks the idle balances available in the Strategy. If either idle balance is less than the calculated withdrawal amount, it falls back to calling IStrategy(strategy).withdrawPartial(shares, totalSupply).

Liquidity Removal Slippage:
In withdrawPartial, the Strategy removes liquidity proportionally from the main and secondary positions. Because liquidity removal from a Uniswap V3 pool is subject to the current pool state—including potential price shifts, slippage, and fees—the amounts recovered from liquidity removal (m0, m1, s0, s1) can be lower than the theoretical amounts. This effect is amplified in volatile markets or when nearly all assets are deployed, leaving minimal idle balances.


### Internal Pre-conditions

The Vault first calls IStrategy(strategy).collectFees() to update fee accruals.

It retrieves total underlying balances via IStrategy(strategy).balances().

The withdrawal amounts are computed as a proportion of the total supply.

The Vault then fetches idle balances via IStrategy(strategy).idleBalances().

If either idle balance is insufficient compared to the calculated withdrawal amount, the Vault calls withdrawPartial.



### External Pre-conditions

High Liquidity Deployment:
Most assets are deployed as liquidity, leaving very low idle balances.

Volatile Market Conditions:
Rapid price movements or high slippage during liquidity removal can cause the recovered amounts from Uniswap positions to be lower than expected.

Timing of Withdrawal:
If users initiate withdrawals during periods when the pool’s state is rapidly changing, the forced liquidity removal process is more likely to occur at suboptimal prices.


### Attack Path

Scenario Setup

Assumptions:

Total Vault shares: 1,000 shares.

Total Strategy balances: 1,000 token0 and 1,000 token1.

A user holds 100 shares (i.e. 10% of the Vault).

Expected withdrawal amounts: 100 token0 and 100 token1.

Idle balances in the Strategy: 50 token0 and 50 token1 (i.e. 5% of the total), because 95% of assets are deployed as liquidity.


Withdrawal Trigger:

The Vault computes the expected withdrawal amounts using the formula:
```solidity 
withdrawAmount0 = totalBalance0 * shares / totalSupply = 1,000 * 100 / 1,000 = 100 token0
withdrawAmount1 = 100 token1
```
It then checks idle balances and finds:

`idle0 = 50 token0 (50 < 100) and idle1 = 50 token1 (50 < 100)`

The condition if (idle0 < withdrawAmount0 || idle1 < withdrawAmount1) triggers the call to:
```solidity 
(withdrawAmount0, withdrawAmount1) = IStrategy(strategy).withdrawPartial(100, 1000)

```

WithdrawPartial Execution

Inside withdrawPartial:

The Strategy calculates the liquidity to remove from the main position:

`liqToRemove_main = mainPosition.liquidity * 100 / 1,000.`

Suppose the main position has 1,000,000 liquidity. Then:

liqToRemove_main = 100,000 liquidity.

Due to market conditions (slippage, fees, price impact), removing 100,000 liquidity might only return, for example, 80 token0 and 80 token1.

Similarly, from the secondary position (e.g., 500,000 liquidity), removing liquidity might yield an additional 10 token0 and 10 token1.

The total tokens recovered would then be:
```solidity 
amount0Out = 80 + 10 + (idle0 * 100/1,000) = 90 + 5 = 95 token0,
amount1Out = 80 + 10 + (idle1 * 100/1,000) = 95 token1.
```

Resulting Impact:

Instead of the expected 100 tokens each, the user receives 95 tokens—representing a 5% shortfall due solely to forced liquidity removal.

In more volatile conditions, this discrepancy could be even higher, leading to a significant loss relative to user expectations.



### Impact

Financial Loss for Users:
Users withdrawing during high liquidity deployment or volatile periods may receive considerably fewer tokens than expected. This “slippage” acts as an unintended penalty.

User Trust and Protocol Reputation:
When users notice that withdrawals do not match the proportional share calculations, it can undermine confidence in the protocol’s fairness and accuracy.

Potential for Systematic Exploitation:
Even if not directly exploitable for theft, repeated forced liquidity removals under adverse market conditions can erode the value of user deposits over time.
