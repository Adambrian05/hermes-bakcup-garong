# Leverager Swap Slippage Manipulation Due to Missing Deadline Checks in Leverager::openLeveragedPosition

## Description
The `openLeveragedPosition` function performs `exactOutput` swaps via an external Uniswap V3 router (e.g., `IMainnetRouter`) to convert borrowed funds into vault tokens (`token0` or `token1)`. These swaps use user-provided `swapParams` (decoded as `IMainnetRouter.ExactOutputParams`), but the contract does not enforce deadline checks or validate the deadline field against the current block timestamp. Additionally, there’s no explicit slippage protection beyond the user-specified `amountInMaximum`, which isn’t strictly enforced or bounded by reasonable limits.

## Vulnerability Detail
https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L103
- `swapParams` includes deadline, but it’s not checked against `block.timestamp`.

- `amountInMaximum` is user-defined without additional validation or slippage bounds.

Swaps are executed without reverting if the input amount exceeds a reasonable threshold, exposing the transaction to MEV manipulation.

This lack of deadline enforcement and slippage control allows searchers or miners to front-run or sandwich the swap, manipulating pool prices to force the contract to spend more of the borrowed denomination asset than intended, potentially resulting in undercollateralized leveraged positions.

## Impact
- MEV Exploitation: A searcher can:
1. Front-run the `openLeveragedPosition` transaction by manipulating the pool (e.g., swapping to inflate the price of token0 or token1).

2. Let the contract execute the `exactOutput` swap at an unfavorable rate, consuming excessive denomination (up to `amountInMaximum`).

3. Back-run to restore the price, profiting from the arbitrage.

Without a deadline, the transaction remains valid in the mempool, increasing the window for such attacks.


-  If the swap consumes more denomination than expected, the resulting vault deposit may not cover the borrowed amount plus interest, leaving the position undercollateralized. 

## Proof of Concept
**Consider this scenario in a WBTC/USDC pool:**
1. User calls `openLeveragedPosition`:
   - Borrows 10 WBTC (denomination).
   - Needs 100k USDC (`token1`) for vault deposit (`a1 = 100_000e6, lp.amount1In = 0`).
   - `swapParams2`: path = WBTC → USDC, `amountOut = 100_000e6`, `amountInMaximum = 20` WBTC, no deadline check.
2. Searcher front-runs:
 - Swaps 5 WBTC → USDC, pushing the price up (e.g., 1 WBTC = 20k USDC → 1 WBTC = 25k USDC).
3. Contract executes:
   - Needs 100k USDC, spends ~12.5 WBTC (instead of ~10 WBTC at fair price).
   - Deposits into vault, but collateral value (in WBTC terms) is now lower than borrowed amount plus interest.
4. Searcher back-runs:
  - Reverses swap, profits ~2.5 WBTC.
 
Result: Position opens with ~7.5 WBTC effective collateral (after repayment) against 10 WBTC debt, potentially undercollateralized.



## Recommendation
-  Validate `swapParams.deadline` against `block.timestamp`.
