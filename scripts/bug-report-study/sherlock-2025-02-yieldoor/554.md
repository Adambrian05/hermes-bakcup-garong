# `Leverager.openLeveragedPosition()` Reverts if Insufficient `Token0` or `Token1` in the `LendingPool`

### Summary

In the `Leverager.openLeveragedPosition()` function, the user first retrieves the necessary `token0` and `token1` from the `LendingPool` and deposits them into the vault. Subsequently, they borrow the `denomination` token, swap it for `token0` and `token1`, and finally return the borrowed `token0` and `token1`. However, if there is insufficient `token0` or `token1` in the `LendingPool` during the initial step, the transaction will revert. A more efficient approach would be to borrow the `denomination` first and then swap it for the required `token0` and `token1`, making the pre-retrieval of these tokens unnecessary.

### Root Cause

Let’s follow the flow of the `openLeveragedPosition()` function:

1. At lines 121-122, the function [retrieves](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L121-L122) the necessary `token0` and `token1` from the `LendingPool` and deposits them into the vault.
2. At line 135, it borrows the `denomination` token from the `LendingPool` and swaps it for `token0` and `token1`.
3. At lines 168-169, it returns the borrowed `token0` and `token1`.

If there are insufficient amounts of `token0` or `token1` in the `LendingPool`, the transaction will revert at the first step. A more appropriate approach would be to borrow the `denomination` first and then swap it for the required `token0` and `token1`, rendering the initial retrieval unnecessary.

Additionally, attackers can create this situation by opening positions while borrowing `token0`, thereby leaving no `token0` available in the `LendingPool` before the user’s transaction. As a result, the user's transaction reverts. Then, the attacker closes their positions.
