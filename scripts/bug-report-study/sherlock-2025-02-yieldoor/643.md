# Lack of Liquidity Buffer in yToken.sol Leads to Capital Inefficiency and Potential Redeeming and Borrowing Delays

### Summary

The current design of the lending pool does not maintain a designated liquidity buffer, leading to capital inefficiency and potential redeeming or borrowing delays during high-demand periods. Instead of dynamically managing liquidity, all deposited or repaid funds are stored in yToken.sol, causing idle capital accumulation and missed yield opportunities. Implementing a liquidity buffer mechanism would optimize capital efficiency and ensure better handling of withdrawals and borrows.

### Root Cause

- The system does not separate an optimal liquidity buffer from yield-bearing reserves.

- Instead, all deposited and repaid funds are stored in yToken.sol, making them available for immediate redemption or borrowing but failing to generate yield when idle.

- There is no mechanism to deploy excess funds to external yield-generating vaults (e.g., Curve, Aave, Compound) when liquidity is above a safe threshold.

- If a large number of users simultaneously withdraw or borrow, [availableLiquidity()](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/libraries/ReserveLogic.sol#L36-L38) might not be sufficient, denying the calls.

### Internal Pre-conditions

1. A user deposits assets into the lending pool.

2. yToken.sol holds the full balance without allocating excess liquidity to a yield-generating strategy.

3. Another user requests a large withdrawal via `redeem()` or the Leverager attempts a flash loan, but available liquidity is insufficient due to the absence of an actively managed buffer.

### External Pre-conditions

1. Market conditions change, causing a spike in withdrawal/borrowing requests.

2. No external liquidity sources (such as vaults) are available to quickly provide additional liquidity.

3. The protocol has idle capital that could have been earning yield instead of sitting unused.

### Impact

- Missed Yield Opportunities: Funds remain idle in yToken.sol instead of being deployed to yield-bearing vaults. This is one essential avenue that could make up for losses arising from bad debts.

- `redeem()` and `borrow()` Denial: In periods of high redemptions/borrows, users may experience liquidity shortages and be denied the calls.

- Reduced Competitiveness: The protocol earns less yield than alternative lending platforms, making it less attractive to depositors.

### Mitigation

To optimize capital efficiency and prevent withdrawal/borrow delays, the protocol should:

1. Implement a Liquidity Buffer Strategy: Define a desired  buffer (percentage threshold) for `reserve.availableLiquidity()`. If the yToken contract balance of the asset exceeds the buffer, deposit the excess into a yield-generating vault.

2. Automatic Liquidity Rebalancing:

- On `deposit()` and `repay()`, check if liquidity exceeds the buffer and allocate excess funds to a yield strategy.

- On `redeem()` or `borrow()`, withdraw from the yield vault if the requested asset amount is less than the contract asset balance in yToken.sol.

- Emergency Withdraw Mechanism: Ensure manual intervention is not required by setting up an automated function to withdraw from vaults when necessary.
