# A malicious first depositor can break the initial pricing in the vault to scare away depositors and earn from it.

### Summary

A malicious first depositor can deposit both assets in the wrong ratio, this will break the asset requirement for legitimate users to deposit into the vault and discourage depositors.  

For example;
1. Vault: USDT-WBTC.
2. Real Price: 1 WBTC = 100,000 USDT.
3. The attacker deposits 0.01 of WBTC and  1 wei USDT.
4. The current price of the Vault is 1 WBTC = 0.00001 USDT (Very far from the market price).
5. Subsequent depositors must deposit 100000 BTC for 1 USDT.

### Root Cause

The root cause is that the first depositor dictates the ratio in which assets are deposited into the vault, and the first depositor can be anyone, including a malicious actor. The pricing ratio depends on the balance of the strategy.

### Internal Pre-conditions

1. Attacker must be the first depositor

### External Pre-conditions

No

### Attack Path

1. Vault: USDT-WBTC.
2. Real Price: 1WBTC = 100000USDT.
3. The attacker deposits 1000 wei of WBTC and  1 wei USDT.
4. The current price of the Vault is 1 WBTC = 0.00001 USDT (Very far from the market price).
5. Subsequent depositors must deposit 100000 WBTC for 1 USDT. (This is already a bad situation)

The protocol owners can attempt to fix this and correct the price by sending USDT directly to the strategy.

6. The vault currently holds 0.01 WBTC (worth $1000) and 10^-6 USDT, to correct the wrong price the protocol has to send 1000 USDT.
7. This will give the attacker access to an extra $1000.

### Impact

1. DOS of the Vault.
2. A form of ransomware on the vault and the protocol owner has to rescue the vault by sending funds to it.
