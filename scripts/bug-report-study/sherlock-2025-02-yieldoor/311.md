# Incorrect Tick Calculation Due to Rounding Errors in Position Width

### Summary

The `Strategy.sol::_setMainTicks` function miscalculates the tick range when `positionWidth` is an odd number due to Solidity's integer division truncation. This results in incorrect tick placement, causing liquidity positions to be misaligned with the expected price range. As a consequence, liquidity providers (LPs) may suffer inefficient capital allocation, reduced fee earnings, and unexpected impermanent loss.

### Root Cause

The issue arises due to integer division in Solidity, which truncates decimal values instead of rounding them properly. When positionWidth is an odd number, dividing it by 2 (positionWidth / 2) results in truncation, leading to inaccurate tick placement.

Additionally, the tick alignment logic (tick - modulo and tickBorder ± halfWidth) does not account for potential rounding inconsistencies, which can cause liquidity positions to be misaligned or incorrectly spaced.

### Attack Path

1. Liquidity Misalignment:

The protocol calculates the main position’s tick range using `_setMainTicks(int24 tick)`.
Due to rounding errors when `positionWidth` is an odd number, the calculated `tickLower` and `tickUpper` values may be off by one tick.

2. Incorrect Positioning:

If the position is misaligned, it may not fully capture expected trading activity, reducing its efficiency in collecting fees.
The position could also be placed outside an optimal range, leading to suboptimal liquidity provisioning.

3. Potential Arbitrage Risks:

If the tick misalignment leads to a gap in liquidity, arbitrage traders may exploit price inefficiencies.
Attackers could manipulate tick movements to push the protocol’s liquidity out of range, causing unnecessary swaps or slippage.

4. Unintended Fee Distribution Issues:

Because the position is misaligned, fee collection mechanisms might not function as expected, leading to losses for liquidity providers.

### Impact

1. Liquidity Inefficiency
If the tick range is incorrectly placed (117-123 instead of 116-124), the position misses out on part of the active trading range.
This means that the protocol earns fewer fees because some liquidity is outside the optimal trading range.
2. Loss of Trading Fees
Assume $1,000,000 in liquidity is misallocated.
If the pool typically earns 0.3% fees per trade, and daily volume is $5,000,000, the correct position would earn:
5,000,000 × 0.003 = 15,000 USDC/day
5,000,000×0.003=15,000 USDC/day
If 10% of the liquidity is misplaced, then $100,000 is not actively earning fees:
100,000 / 1,000,000 × 15,000 = 1,500 USDC lost per day

Over one month, the protocol loses:
1,500 × 30 = 45,000 USDC

3. Compounding Errors Over Time
If `_setMainTicks` is called multiple times with different odd `positionWidth` values, errors accumulate, further worsening liquidity placement.
Over time, millions in fees could be lost.

### Mitigation

Modify this line:

```solidity
int24 halfWidth = int24(positionWidth / 2);
```

To this:

```solidity
int24 halfWidth = int24((positionWidth + 1) / 2);
```
