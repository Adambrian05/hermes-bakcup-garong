# No slippage check in `addLiquidityToMainPosition()`

### Summary
The `_addLiquidityToMainPosition` function in the Strategy contract is vulnerable to sandwich attacks and adverse liquidity deployment due to the absence of a slippage check. The function assumes that the price state of the Uniswap V3 pool remains stable between transaction initiation and execution, but this assumption fails under real-world conditions where price movements can significantly alter execution outcomes.

While the `rebalance()` and `compound()` functions attempt to mitigate this risk by checking price deviations, these checks are insufficient in protecting liquidity providers from MEV (Maximal Extractable Value) attacks, particularly sandwich attacks.

### Vulnerability Details
The function calls `LiquidityAmounts.getLiquidityForAmounts()` to determine the liquidity to be minted

```solidity
uint128 liquidity = LiquidityAmounts.getLiquidityForAmounts(
    sqrtPriceX96,
    TickMath.getSqrtRatioAtTick(tickLower),
    TickMath.getSqrtRatioAtTick(tickUpper),
    amount0,
    amount1
);
```

However, there is no check to ensure that the minted liquidity is above a reasonable threshold relative to the input tokens. If an attacker artificially moves the price just before execution, liquidity can be significantly lower than what was originally expected.

If liquidity minted is disproportionately low relative to `amount0` and `amount1`, the transaction will still execute, leaving excessive “remaining” tokens unutilized.

The function `_requirePriceWithinRange()` checks whether the TWAP is within an acceptable range, as called in `rebalance()`

This check, however, occurs before the liquidity addition, leaving a time gap where an attacker can manipulate price in a way that worsens execution conditions.

An MEV bot or malicious liquidity provider can front-run the rebalance or compound transaction by executing a swap that pushes the pool price to an extreme value.

When `_addLiquidityToMainPosition` executes, the adversely affected exchange rate causes the protocol to mint significantly less liquidity for the same amount of input tokens. Immediately after the transaction, the attacker executes another swap to restore the price back to normal, pocketing a risk-free profit at the expense of the protocol’s liquidity efficiency

### Impact
The protocol mints significantly less liquidity than expected. This inefficiency means that, at that moment, a portion of the user’s funds isn’t earning fees or contributing to the position’s exposure. Even if these idle tokens are later re-added during a rebalance, they missed out on earning yield during the interim

### Recommendations
Introduce a slippage check into` _addLiquidityToMainPosition`
