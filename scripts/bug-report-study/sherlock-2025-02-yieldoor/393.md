# Direct Transfer of Underlying Assets to yToken Contract Affects Reserve Accounting

### Summary

The direct transfer of underlying assets to the yToken contract without proper accounting updates will cause an imbalance in reserve tracking for the lending protocol as attackers can exploit the discrepancy to redeem yTokens for a disproportionate share of underlying tokens.

### Root Cause

In LendingPool.sol, the function responsible for deposits transfers assets directly to the yToken contract without ensuring that the underlyingBalance is properly updated if tokens are sent outside of the deposit function.

### Internal Pre-conditions

A user or contract sends underlying tokens directly to the yToken contract without using the deposit function.

The LendingPool relies on underlyingBalance for reserve tracking instead of reading the actual balance.

The discrepancy between actual token balance and underlyingBalance exists due to the missing update mechanism.

### External Pre-conditions

No external contract updates the LendingPool’s reserve balance after direct transfers.

Market conditions allow arbitrage or exploitation of the incorrect exchange rate.

### Attack Path

The attacker directly transfers underlying tokens to the yToken contract, bypassing the deposit function.

The LendingPool’s underlyingBalance remains unchanged, creating a discrepancy between recorded and actual reserves.

The attacker calls redeem() to withdraw more underlying tokens than they should receive, exploiting the incorrect exchange rate calculation.

Repeating this process allows the attacker to drain funds at the expense of legitimate depositors.

### Impact

This vulnerability results in incorrect reserve tracking and an exploitable exchange rate mechanism. Adversaries can withdraw more than their fair share of underlying assets, leading to financial losses for depositors and potential destabilization of the protocol’s reserves.
