# Liquidations can be reverted by malicious swaps over the slippage

### Summary

Liquidations can be reverted by malicious swaps over the slippage

### Root Cause

When liquidating, we have this line:
```solidity
(uint256 amount0, uint256 amount1) = IVault(up.vault).withdraw(up.shares, liqParams.minAmount0, liqParams.minAmount1);
```
We withdraw from the vault which withdraws liquidity from a Univ3 pool (if there are not enough idle assets which would be in most cases, especially for large positions). The issue is that the one being liquidated can simply swap in such a way so that the slippage provider by the liquidator kicks in and the transaction reverts.

### Internal Pre-conditions

_No_

### External Pre-conditions

_No_

### Attack Path

1. Bob will liquidate Alice, the position is 100 token0 and 100 token1 and he wants 90 of each as a minimum
2. Alice swaps in the pool and manipulates the prices in such a way that Bob receives 80 tokens of one of the tokens and more of the other one
3. Transaction reverts and Alice is not liquidated
4. This is a time-sensitive operation and can lead to bad debt

### Impact

Reverts on time-sensitive liquidations
