# Flawed Leverage Calculation Allows Extreme Overleveraging and Lending Pool Draining Leading to Rapid Liquidation

### **Summary**
A **flawed leverage formula** in `_checkWithinlimits()` allows **users to bypass leverage restrictions**, enabling them to **borrow far beyond the intended 2x limit**, potentially reaching e.g. **20x leverage** and technically infinitely beyond instead. This **puts positions at the edge of liquidation**, meaning **any small price drop instantly triggers mass liquidations**. The protocol may **not be able to liquidate these positions fast enough**, causing **bad debt and severe liquidity risks for the LendingPool and Vaults**.

Note: The writing of this report is based off the doc's example where max leverage allowed is 2x for simplicity.

## **Root Cause**
- In **Leverager.sol**, [`_checkWithinlimits()`](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L464) contains the following incorrect leverage formula:
  
  ```solidity
  uint256 positionLeverage = (up.initCollateralValue + up.borrowedAmount) * 1e18 / up.initCollateralValue;
  ```
  - This calculation **fails to properly account for borrowed funds**, allowing a user to **borrow significantly more than intended**.
  - The correct formula should be:
  
  ```solidity
  uint256 positionLeverage = up.initCollateralValue * 1e18 / (up.initCollateralValue - up.borrowedAmount);
  ```
  - This ensures leverage is **calculated based on net collateral** rather than artificially inflated values.
  
  **Contradiction Breakdown:**
  - The flawed formula **treats borrowing as self-canceling**, artificially lowering leverage as more funds are borrowed.
  - Instead of the **expected 2x limit**, users can **open positions at 20x leverage**, far exceeding safe thresholds.
  - A **tiny market drop will instantly liquidate these high-leverage positions**, leading to **chaotic liquidations and potential bad debt**.

## **Internal Pre-conditions**
1. A **leveraged user** calls `openLeveragedPosition()`.
2. `_checkWithinlimits()` fails to properly restrict leverage due to the flawed formula.
3. User **borrows much more than the intended 2x cap**, **exposing the protocol to extreme risk**.

## **External Pre-conditions**
1. **LendingPool must have available liquidity** for borrowing.
2. **Market must be stable enough** for [`checkPoolActivity()`](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Strategy.sol#L308-L342) to pass, allowing the leveraged position to open.

## **Attack Path / Vulnerability Path**
1. A user **provides $100 collateral**.
2. Due to the flawed formula, instead of being allowed borrowing the **intended $100 to make it a $200 position**, the user **borrows $1,900**, resulting in **a $2000 position that is 20x leverage** instead of **2x**.

First off, `a0` and `a1` as returned by [`Vault.deposit()`](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Vault.sol#L46-L47) is the actual amounts of `token0` and `token1` the user deposited into the Vault via `openLeveragedPositio()`, and they are already inclusive of `delta0` and `delta1` [flashloaned](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L121-L122) albeit to be corrected later on for the actual amount of denominated tokens borrowed from the Lending Pool.

https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L129-L131

```solidity
        up.initCollateralUsd = _calculateTokenValues(up.token0, up.token1, a0, a1, price); // returns the USD price in 1e18
        uint256 bPrice = IPriceFeed(pricefeed).getPrice(lp.denomination);
        up.initCollateralValue = up.initCollateralUsd * (10 ** ERC20(lp.denomination).decimals()) / bPrice;
```
As such, `up.initCollateralValue` is the total of the user's and borrowed collateral value. Unfortunately, it's being misused as follows making the [`positionLeverage` check](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L467), i.e. less than 2x, to pass: 

positionLeverage = ((100 + 1900) + 1900) / (100 + 1900) = 1.95

Note: The user can actually borrow astronomically and drain Lending Pool liquidity if not because of the required [`maxIndividualBorrow`](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L469) or [`isLiquidateable`](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L411) check. 

Per the doc explanation and its example below, the user's position could be leveraged to $2000 value ($1900 borrowed, $100 collateral) before it would become liquidateable with a slight market drop:  

> To put it into a numerical example, user opens up a $1,250 position with $1,000 of collateral (1.25x leverage). User’s position would have to fall below $275 value ($250 borrowed, $25 collateral) in order to become liquidateable. 

3. **If the market drops even slightly (e.g., 1-2%),** the position immediately becomes liquidatable.
4. **Liquidation bots attempt to execute liquidations**, but if too many users abuse this flaw at once, **the protocol may not be able to keep up with liquidations**.
5. **If liquidations fail or occur too slowly, the LendingPool suffers bad debt**, making **funds unavailable for lenders**.
6. Additionally, [`vaultParams[up.vault].maxCumulativeBorrowedUSD`](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L470-L473) limit check could also be reached sooner than expected, making the leveraging inaccessible to new users.

## **Impact**
- **Users can abuse the flaw to borrow far beyond the intended limits**, taking on **excessive risk**. Users would only need to risk a small collateral fund of their own to bet on big returns through the Vault if market moves in their favor, but if not just a small loss.
- **If the market moves against them, mass liquidations occur instantly**, overwhelming liquidation bots.
- **Bad debts may pile up**, **harming protocol solvency and exposing the protocol to systemic risk**.
- **Lenders may be unable to redeem**, as the users' collateral backing the leveraged positions along with the underlying assets is now stuck in the underwater liquidated positions as bad debts.
- **This completely contradicts the expected behavior of the protocol**, which should **cap leverage at 2x as per documentation**.

## **Mitigation**
1. **Fix `_checkWithinlimits()` leverage formula**:

https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Leverager.sol#L464

   ```diff
-        uint256 positionLeverage = (up.initCollateralValue + up.borrowedAmount) * 1e18 / up.initCollateralValue;
+        uint256 positionLeverage = up.initCollateralValue * 1e18 / (up.initCollateralValue - up.borrowedAmount);
   ```
2. **Implement additional liquidation safeguards** to prevent positions from being too close to the liquidation threshold upon opening.
3. **Enforce stricter leverage caps and rate-limit borrowing activity** to prevent cascading liquidations.
