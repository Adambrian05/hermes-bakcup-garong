# Potential Precision Loss in Vault Contract Due to Double Division

## Summary

In the[ `_calcDeposit` ](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/b5a0f779dce4236b02665606adb610099451a51a/yieldoor/src/Vault.sol#L119-L151)function of the [`Vault`](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Vault.sol) contract, there is a potential precision loss due to consecutive division operations when calculating `shares`. Specifically, the issue arises in the following line:

```solidity
shares = cross * totalSupply / bal0 / bal1;
```

Since Solidity performs integer division, dividing twice in succession may lead to unintended rounding errors, potentially under-allocating shares to depositors.

## Root Cause 

The consecutive division operations in the `_calcDeposit` function cause precision loss due to Solidity’s integer division behavior. Since Solidity truncates instead of rounding, dividing twice in succession amplifies rounding errors, leading to under-allocation of shares for depositors.

**In [`Vault.sol: _calcDeposit()`](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Vault.sol),** the calculation:
```solidity
shares = cross * totalSupply / bal0 / bal1;

```


introduces unnecessary truncation because:

1. `cross * totalSupply / bal0` truncates before the next division.
2. The subsequent division `/ bal1` further compounds precision loss.
3. This results in depositors receiving fewer shares than expected.


## Attack Path 

an **adversarial scenario** could arise where a sophisticated user deposits funds repeatedly in small increments, leveraging the precision loss to extract a marginal advantage.

**Potential Exploit Path:**

1. A user calls `deposit()` multiple times with carefully selected amounts.
2. Due to rounding errors, small deposits might be allocated slightly more shares per deposit than a single large deposit.
3. Over time, this user could accumulate a slightly higher share of the vault than intended at the expense of other depositors.


## **Impact:**

- **Depositors suffer precision loss**, meaning they receive fewer shares than expected for their deposits.
- **A potential precision arbitrage opportunity** exists, where a user could deposit funds in smaller transactions to receive slightly more shares cumulatively.
- **Vault users may experience reduced returns** over time, especially when large deposits are involved.

## Proof of Concept (PoC)


The precision loss in `_calcDeposit` occurs due to consecutive integer divisions in Solidity. Since Solidity truncates decimal values instead of rounding, repeated division amplifies precision loss.

The original formula:

shares = (cross * totalSupply) / bal0 / bal1;

By dividing twice in succession, we risk truncating significant portions of the result, especially with large values.

Let’s consider larger numbers where truncation can have a severe impact: 
- `cross = 10^18` 
- `totalSupply = 5 × 10^17`
- `bal0 = 3 × 10^12`
- `bal1 = 7 × 10^12` 

#### **Current Computation (Problematic Order)** 
Using the original formula:

shares = ((10^18 * 5 * 10^17) / (3 * 10^12)) / (7 * 10^12);

1. **First division:**
(10^18 * 5 * 10^17) / (3 * 10^12) = (5 * 10^35) / (3 * 10^12) = 1.6666 × 10^23

Since Solidity truncates, this becomes: 1.6666 × 10^23 (Truncated, losing decimal precision)


2. **Second division:**
(1.6666 × 10^23) / (7 × 10^12) = 2.3809 × 10^10

Again, Solidity truncates the remainder, further compounding the precision loss.


#### **Expected Computation (Reordered for Precision)** 
Using the corrected formula:

shares = (10^18 * 5 * 10^17) / (3 * 10^12 * 7 * 10^12);

1. **Multiplication first:**  (3 * 10^12) * (7 * 10^12) = 21 × 10^24
2. **Final division:** (5 × 10^35) / (21 × 10^24) = 2.3809523 × 10^10


**Key Difference:** 
- The problematic order truncates to **2.3809 × 10¹⁰** 
- The corrected formula keeps **2.3809523 × 10¹⁰**, **preserving more precision**




### Mitigation 

#### **1. Reorder Operations to Prioritize Multiplication**

Since Solidity truncates decimals in division, **always perform multiplications before divisions** to minimize precision loss.

```solidity
shares = (cross * totalSupply) / (bal0 * bal1);
```

This ensures that division occurs only once, reducing rounding errors.
