# Malicious user can sandwich every time `_requirePriceWithinRange` is called and Dos the `compound` & `rebalance` functions.

## Summary 

`Strategy::compound` & `Strategy::rebalance` functions calls `_requirePriceWithinRange` to check the volatility of of the `UniswapV3 Pool`. Attacker can potentially front run every time `rebalancer` calls `compound` & `rebalance` function to swap a huge amount from the pool to manipulate the pool and deny  `compound` & `rebalance` because `_requirePriceWithinRange` check reverts and the funds in the protocol sit idly.
[Reference](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/Strategy.sol#L201)

## Attack Path

- Users deposit funds in the protocol.
- `rebalancer` calls `compound` or `rebalance` to manage the funds.
- Attacker front runs the `compound` or `rebalance` using flash loans to swap a large amount and manipulate the prices.
- `rebalancer` function gets through the mempool and `_requirePriceWithinRange` it checks for the volatility or price manipulation and it reverts the call.
- Attacker swaps back and returns the loan
- And the protocol's funds are not managed as intended.

## Impact 

- Denial of service: This disrupts the protocol's operations and can lead to financial losses for users.
- Users can suffer losses due to the protocol's inability to rebalance or compound.
- The attack can be used to manipulate prices, creating opportunities for financial gain through shorting the protocol shares.
- The attack can severely damage the protocol's reputation, eroding user confidence and trust.
- This attack can be easily automated, making it more dangerous.

## Mitigation

1. Private Transactions:

- Send transactions directly to miners or validators, bypassing the public mempool. This prevents front-runners from observing and copying your transactions.
- Tools like Flashbots Protect or services that support private transaction submission can be used

2. Circuit Breakers:

- Automatic Pausing: Implement circuit breakers that automatically pause the rebalance or compound functions if repeated failures occur or if significant price deviations are detected.
- Threshold-Based Triggers: Define clear thresholds for price deviations or failure rates that trigger the circuit breakers.
- Manual Override: Provide the protocol owner or a governance mechanism with the ability to manually activate or deactivate the circuit breakers.
