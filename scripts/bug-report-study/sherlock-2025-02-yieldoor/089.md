# Lending Pool interest rate taylor approximation actually overcharges borrowers

### Summary

`InterestRateUtils::calculateCompoundedInterest()` does the [following](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-02-yieldoor/blob/main/yieldoor/src/libraries/InterestRateUtils.sol#L64) approximation:
> (1+x)^n = 1+n*x+[n/2*(n-1)]*x^2+[n/6*(n-1)*(n-2)*x^3...
> The approximation slightly underpays liquidity providers and undercharges borrowers

However, this is only true for a big enough `n`. For many values of `n`, such as 4 hours (`n` is the time passed since the last interaction with the lending pool), the approximation actually overcharges borrowers, harming them. Loss exceeds 10 USD in 2 years and 9 million in 10 years. An attacker can easily trigger this, the mentioned chains are cheap.


### Root Cause

In `InterestRateUtils:73`, the approximation used overcharges borrowers.

### Internal Pre-conditions

None.

### External Pre-conditions

None.

1. Borrowers are organically overcharged.

### Impact

Although this is not stated as an invariant, it causes losses for borrowers who are overcharged and is worthy of medium severity.

If interactions with the pool are every 4 hours or similar, the calculation overcharges borrowers. An attacker can on purpose trigger this to overcharge borrowers.
