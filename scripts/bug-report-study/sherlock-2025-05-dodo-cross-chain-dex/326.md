# Slippage does not consider amount after `gasFee`

## Vulnerability Details

In the `GatewayTransferNative.withdrawToNativeChain` function, there is insufficient slippage protection against the fluctuating gas fees imposed by Zetachain. This could result in users receiving less than the anticipated amount on the destination chain.

For the flow from `GatewayTransferNative.withdrawToNativeChain` to `GatewayTransferNative._handleBitcoinWithdraw`, slippage is necessary to safeguard against the variable gas fees obtained from the Zetachain system contract.

Similarly, for the flow from `GatewayTransferNative.withdrawToNativeChain` to `GatewayTransferNative._handleEvmOrSolanaWithdraw`, slippage is essential to protect against the unpredictable gas fees sourced from the Zetachain system contract, as well as any modifications made by the admin to the static `gasLimit`.

The  call flows will obtain gas fees from one of the following two functions in the `GatewayZEVM` contract.

## POC

Consider the following scenario

1. Alice intends to transfer 100 USDC.ETH to her Ethereum receiver address using `GatewayTransferNative.withdrawToNativeChain`, which leads her into `GatewayTransferNative._handlEvmOrSolanaWithdraw`, where she observes that the gasLimit is set to 5e6. Let's assume the gas costs 1 USDC.ETH in gasZRC20 tokens.
2. Alice's transaction is currently pending execution.
3. In the mean time, gas fees has adjusted to cost 2 USDC.ETH.
4. When Alice's transaction finally executes and enters `GatewayTransferNative._handlEvmOrSolanaWithdraw`, the `gasFee` amounts to 2 USDC.ETH. Although she has sufficient funds to cover this fee, she only sends 98 USDC.ETH, which is then received as USDC at her Ethereum receiver address.
5. Ultimately, she ends up receiving less USDC (98 vs 99) than she originally intended

## Root Cause

Lack of slippage protection for users regarding volatile gas fees

## Impact

Loss of funds for user

## Mitigation

Consider introducing a slippage parameter to protect user against volatile gas fees.
