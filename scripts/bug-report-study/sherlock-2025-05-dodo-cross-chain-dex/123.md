# Hard-coded Uniswap addresses will **halt swaps** for GatewayTransferNative users

### Summary

Hard-coding `UniswapRouter` and `UniswapFactory` as immutable constants will cause **cross-chain swap failures** for GatewayTransferNative users, as a router/factory migration will direct every swap call to a non-functional contract.

### Root Cause

*In `GatewayTransferNative.sol`

https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-05-dodo-cross-chain-dex/blob/main/omni-chain-contracts/contracts/GatewayTransferNative.sol#L23-L24

The router (`UniswapRouter`) and factory (`UniswapFactory`) addresses are declared `public constant`, baking them into the bytecode. Any call to `IUniswapV2Router01(UniswapRouter)` or `UniswapV2Library.getAmountsIn(...)` therefore **cannot be redirected** if the addresses are wrong or later superseded.

### Internal Pre-conditions

1. Uniswap later upgrades / deprecates the router or factory on that same chain.

### External Pre-conditions

1. Uniswap Labs (or the dominant liquidity fork on that chain) **publishes a new router/factory** and front-ends/liquidity migrate to it, **or**
2. The original router/factory is paused, self-destructed, or otherwise becomes non-operational.

However, the chance that the specific address stops working is lower because liquidity and front ends rarely abandon the previous router overnight.

### Attack Path
N/A

### Impact

All swap-dependent functions **revert**, preventing cross-chain exits and potentially trapping user assets until an implementation upgrade is executed. No direct fund theft, but **complete service outage** for affected users (value at risk ≈ total live liquidity interacting with the contract).

### Mitigation

* **Configurable addresses:** Add `setUniswapRouter()` and `setUniswapFactory()` owner-only setters (mirroring the existing DODO setters).
