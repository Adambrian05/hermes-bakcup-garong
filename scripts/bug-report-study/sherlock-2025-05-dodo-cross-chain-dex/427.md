# ncorrect Handling of Native ETH in onRevert() Leads to Unexpected Token Receipt, Fund Lock, and Financial Risk from Volatility

### Summary

The onRevert() function in the GatewaySend contract assumes all assets are ERC20 tokens and uses safeTransfer() to refund users when cross-chain calls fail. When native ETH is involved, this call fails — causing onRevert() to revert.

As a result, ZetaChain falls back to calling onAbort() on ZEVM. The system stores the refund under the original EVM sender address, which may be a contract .

If that contract does not exist , the funds become unrecoverable unless DODO bots intervene .

This leads to:

Users receiving an unexpected token ie receipt token on other chain instead of token they initially wanted to swap to.
Potential fund loss if the sender was a contract with no ZEVM counterpart or forced Manual recovery by bots required via claimRefund() as well as exposure to price volatility even if funds are recovered as it would likely take some time before the funds are approved by the protocol to be recovered by their bots 

for example 
Alice wants to bridge 1 ETH (worth $4,000 ) from Ethereum to ZetaChain and swap it directly to USDC using DODO’s cross-chain integration.

She uses a smart contract wallet to initiate the transaction. The contract is configured to call depositAndCall() with native ETH and encode a swap instruction on ZEVM.

⚠️ What Goes Wrong:
The swap on ZEVM reverts (e.g., slippage too high, pool not found).
ZetaChain triggers onRevert() on Ethereum → fails because safeTransfer would not go through
System falls back to calling onAbort() on ZEVM.
Refund info is stored under Alice’s contract address , which does not exist on ZEVM .
Funds are locked in the refund mapping until DODO bots intervene .

during the period it takes alice to convince the protocol that the funds belong to her, eth price drops to 3900 $ forcing alice to lose 100 $ worth 



### Root Cause

In onRevert(), the system assumes all assets are ERC20 tokens:

1
TransferHelper.safeTransfer(context.asset, sender, context.amount);
When context.asset == address(0) (native ETH), this fails because there’s no code at address(0) to handle .transfer().

This causes onRevert() to revert, triggering a fallback to onAbort() on ZEVM. Refund info is stored using the original EVM sender address , which may be a contract that:

Does not exist on ZEVM,
Or has no logic to claim refunds .
Unless DODO bots recover these funds, they remain stuck.
