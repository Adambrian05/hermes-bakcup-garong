# On-chain slippage setting enables MEV sandwich risk and uncontrolled maxAmountIn

### Summary

Computing `amountInMax` on-chain using a global `slippage` parameter is wrong. An attacker (or MEV bot) can manipulate the pool price before the withdrawal , driving up the input required and causing users to over-spend or fail.

### Root Cause

In `_swapAndSendERC20Tokens()`, the `GatewayCrossChain.sol` contract does: https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-05-dodo-cross-chain-dex/blob/main/omni-chain-contracts/contracts/GatewayCrossChain.sol#L309-L316 . Because `amountsQuote` is fetched and then used in the same transaction, no one outside the contract signs off on that quote or cap. hence whatever the current onchain price is, is what the user would be forced to swap with and there is no protection against any kind of price manipulation 
this would lead to
1. The contract to consume far more `targetZRC20` than intended (value loss).

### Internal Pre-conditions
none
### External Pre-conditions

1. An MEV bot or malicious actor observes the pending transaction.

2. They front-run or sandwich the pool to shift the price away from the quoted rate.



### Attack Path

1. Contract fetches a quote (`getAmountsIn()`) at price P.

2. malicious user shifts price beofre the the call to an unfavourable price 

3. call uses this unfavorable price to swap 


  - Contract spends more than `amountInMax` (due to AMM mechanics), draining extra funds.

### Impact

- Users suffer loss of extra tokens when the AMM price moves between quote and execution.
