# Missing setter for `slippage` in `GatewayCrossChain` / `GatewayTransferNative` can cause protocol to lose fees during volatile market conditions

## Vulnerability Details 

The `GatewayCrossChain` contract lacks a setter function for the `slippage` variable. This omission becomes problematic as the `slippage` variable plays a crucial role in the `GatewayCrossChain.onCall` function, which is responsible for collecting platform fees.

For instance, if the protocol sets the slippage to a fixed value of 1% (slippage = 10), a user trying to swap targetZRC20 tokens for the corresponding gasZRC20 tokens may encounter issues. If the market price shifts to 2% higher than the expected amount of targetZRC20 tokens for the desired gasZRC20 tokens, the transaction will revert.

Although the user may be willing to accept the 2% slippage, the hardcoded slippage in the protocol prevents any modifications, even by the admin. Consequently, this leads to transaction reverts, causing the protocol to forfeit potential platform fees while the user loses gas fees.

## POC

Consider the following scenario

1. Alice successfully initiates a call from an EVM-compatible chain, triggering `GatewayCrossChain.onCall` to send her 100 USDC.ETH to her address on the ZetaChain.
2. Upon entering `GatewayCrossChain.onCall` -> `GatewayCrossChain._handleEvmOrSolanaWithdraw` -> `GatewayCrossChain._swapAndSendERC20Tokens`, the amount of USDC.ETH she needs to swap for 1 gasUSDC is currently 1 USDC.ETH.
3. If the protocol sets a slippage of 1%, this would require 1.01 USDC.ETH.
4. However, during this time, the market price increases by 2%, meaning she now needs 1.02 USDC.ETH to exchange for 1 gasUSDC.
5. Although she is willing to accept this slippage due to the small amount, her transaction will revert because she cannot modify the fixed slippage.
6. Consequently, she forfeits her gas fees, and the protocol loses fees equivalent to a percentage of her principal (100 USDC.ETH) that she intends to send. This loss could be even greater if she sends a larger amount of tokens.

## Root Cause

Absence of a setter for the slippage variable may lead to continuous transaction reverts. The slippage is hardcoded and cannot be modified.

## Impact

Loss of yield for protocol
Broken core functionality when trying to transfer assets to receiver address on zeta chain.

## Mitigation

Include a setter for the variable `slippage` to prevent protocol from missing out on platform fees due to reverted calls
A better design is to allow user to specify the slippage they wish to accept
