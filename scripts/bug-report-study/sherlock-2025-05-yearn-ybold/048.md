# Accountant may take fees before yBOLD price per share (for LPs) returns to 1, violating stated invariant

### Summary

The invariant is:
> "If `process_report` reports a loss, `Accountant.sol` will not take any fees until the yBOLD price per share returns to 1."

This implies that if a vault's Price Per Share (PPS) is below 1 (meaning `vault.totalAssets() / vault.totalSupply()` is less than 1, indicating LPs are effectively at a loss relative to a baseline of 1), no performance fees should be taken from strategy gains until this PPS is restored to at least 1.

However, the fee calculation logic in `Accountant.sol`'s `report` [function ](https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-05-yearn-ybold/blob/main/yv3-liquityv2-sp-strategy/src/periphery/Accountant.sol#L146)can lead to situations where performance fees are charged on a strategy's reported gain, even though the vault LPs' PPS remains below 1 after this gain is realized and fees are deducted.

### Root Cause

The `Accountant.report` function calculates fees based on the reported `gain` from a strategy. The vault calling `report` would have updated its `totalAssets()` to include this `gain` *before* calling the accountant. Let `A_prior` be the vault's total assets before the current strategy's `gain`, `g` be the current strategy's reported `gain`, and `S` be the vault's `totalSupply()`. The `Accountant.report` function reads `assets = A_prior + g`.

The fee logic is approximately:
1.  `totalFees` is tentatively set to `g` (reflecting a 100% performance fee on the current `gain`).
2.  It then reads `currentVaultAssets = vault.totalAssets()` (which is `A_prior + g`) and `currentVaultSupply = vault.totalSupply()` (which is `S`).
3.  If `currentVaultAssets < currentVaultSupply`:
    *   This means that even after incorporating the entire current `gain` (`g`) into the vault's assets (pre-fees), the vault is still "underwater" (PPS < 1).
    *   A `needed` amount is calculated as `needed = currentVaultSupply - currentVaultAssets`. This is the *remaining shortfall* after the current gain `g` has been notionally added.
    *   `totalFees` is then adjusted: `totalFees = (g < needed) ? 0 : (g - needed)`.
4.  Else (if `currentVaultAssets >= currentVaultSupply`):
    *   This means that after incorporating `g`, the vault (pre-fees) is at or above PPS = 1.
    *   `totalFees` remains `g`.

The invariant is violated in the following scenarios where the vault started underwater (`A_prior < S`):

*   **Scenario 1: Vault remains underwater post-gain (pre-fee), but fees are still taken.**
    *   Condition: `A_prior + g < S` (matches point 3 logic) AND `g >= needed`.
    *   `totalFees = g - needed = g - (S - (A_prior + g)) = 2g + A_prior - S`.
    *   If `totalFees > 0`, fees are taken.
    *   The LPs' assets after fees are: `(A_prior + g) - totalFees = (A_prior + g) - (2g + A_prior - S) = S - g`.
    *   The resulting PPS for LPs is `(S - g) / S = 1 - g/S`. If `g > 0`, this PPS is `< 1`.
    *   **Example:** `S=1000`, `A_prior=900` (PPS=0.9). Strategy reports `g=80`.
        *   `currentVaultAssets = 900 + 80 = 980`. This is `< S=1000`.
        *   `needed = 1000 - 980 = 20`.
        *   Since `g=80 >= needed=20`, `totalFees = 80 - 20 = 60`.
        *   LPs' final assets = `980 - 60 = 920`. Final PPS = `920/1000 = 0.92`.
        *   Here, fees (60) were taken, but the LPs' PPS (0.92) did not return to 1.

*   **Scenario 2: Vault recovers to PPS >= 1 post-gain (pre-fee), but full fees on gain leave LPs underwater.**
    *   Condition: `A_prior < S` AND `A_prior + g >= S` (matches point 4 logic).
    *   `totalFees = g`.
    *   The LPs' assets after fees are: `(A_prior + g) - totalFees = (A_prior + g) - g = A_prior`.
    *   The resulting PPS for LPs is `A_prior / S`. Since `A_prior < S` initially, this PPS is `< 1`.
    *   **Example:** `S=1000`, `A_prior=900` (PPS=0.9). Strategy reports `g=150`.
        *   `currentVaultAssets = 900 + 150 = 1050`. This is `>= S=1000`.
        *   `totalFees = g = 150`.
        *   LPs' final assets = `1050 - 150 = 900`. Final PPS = `900/1000 = 0.9`.
        *   Here, fees (150) were taken, but the LPs' PPS (0.9) did not return to 1.

The fundamental issue is that the fee logic does not prioritize making LPs whole (restoring PPS to 1) before taking a significant portion (or all) of the current gain as fees. The check `if (assets < supply)` uses `assets` that *include* the current gain, rather than assessing the shortfall *before* the current gain is applied to cover it.


### Internal Pre-conditions

*   The yBOLD vault (which uses this `Accountant.sol`) has `totalAssets()` effectively less than `totalSupply()` from an LP perspective (i.e., `A_prior < S`), meaning its PPS is below 1 due to accumulated past losses or unrealized gains.
*   A strategy linked to this yBOLD vault reports a `gain > 0` to the vault, which then calls `Accountant.report()`.

### External Pre-conditions

*   Market conditions that might cause initial losses for vault strategies, followed by profitable periods.

### Attack Path

This is not an exploit controllable by an external attacker in the traditional sense. Rather, it's a systemic issue where the fee mechanism operates contrary to a stated invariant or a common expectation of "high-water mark" style fee assessment. The `feeRecipient` is the beneficiary of these "prematurely" taken fees, at the expense of the vault LPs who expect to recover their initial capital (normalized to PPS=1) before performance fees are levied on profits that merely offset prior losses.


### Impact

*   **Loss for LPs:** LPs in the yBOLD vault may pay performance fees on strategy profits even if their overall investment in the vault remains at a loss (PPS < 1). Profits that could have been used to restore LP capital towards the PPS=1 baseline are instead diverted as fees.
*   **Violation of Trust/Expectation:** The behavior contradicts the invariant, potentially eroding trust if LPs expect fees to be deferred until they are "made whole."
*   **Reduced Yield for LPs in Recovery:** During periods where the vault is recovering from previous losses, LPs' effective yield is lower than it would be if fees were only charged on net new profits above the PPS=1 high-water mark.
