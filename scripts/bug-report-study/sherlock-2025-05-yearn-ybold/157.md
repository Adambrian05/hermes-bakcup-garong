# Protocol liquidators will severely underprice collateral auctions, causing significant losses to the yBOLD protocol and its users

### Summary

The incorrect order of arithmetic operations in the auction starting price calculation will cause a significant underpricing of collateral auctions for the yBOLD protocol, leading to economic losses for users and the protocol. This happens as liquidators (or the system) will set auction prices based on truncated and imprecise values, breaking the core invariant that collateral must be liquidated at fair market value or above to cover debts and maintain protocol solvency. As a result, the protocol’s financial safety mechanisms are compromised, increasing the risk of bad debt and reduced user funds.

### Root Cause

https://github.com/sherlock-audit/2025-05-yearn-ybold/blob/main/yv3-liquityv2-sp-strategy/src/Strategy.sol#L241

In the _tend() function of the strategy contract (link to code), the auction starting price calculation performs division before multiplication, causing truncation errors and significant precision loss. Specifically, the expression _available * _price / WAD * _bufferPercentage / WAD / WAD divides too early, reducing intermediate values before multiplying, which leads to underpriced auctions.

This breaks the yBOLD protocol invariant that collateral must be auctioned at fair or above-market value to cover outstanding debts and avoid losses. Because the auction price is underestimated, the protocol risks liquidating collateral at undervalued prices, potentially causing bad debt and losses to stakers and the protocol treasury.

### Internal Pre-conditions

1. The strategy contract must hold a positive balance of collateral tokens (`COLL`) available for auction (i.e., `_available > 0`).
2. The `COLL_PRICE_ORACLE` must return a valid price `_price` for the collateral token (not zero or stale).
3. The auction contract (`AUCTION`) must be initialized and enabled for the collateral token (`COLL`).
4. The `_tend()` function must be triggered by the keeper or strategy at a time when the auction is active or about to be started.
5. The buffer percentage (`bufferPercentage`) used in the price calculation must be set to a non-zero value.
6. The arithmetic operations in `_tend()` rely on integer math with fixed-point decimals (`WAD`), without additional rounding safeguards.

### External Pre-conditions

1. The collateral price oracle (`COLL_PRICE_ORACLE`) must provide a price feed for the collateral token with sufficient precision and frequency.
2. The price feed may experience sudden or large fluctuations in price, affecting the `_price` variable used in auction pricing.
3. The price oracle may switch its price source from primary to a fallback or become temporarily unavailable (oracle down), triggering use of the `ORACLE_DOWN_BUFFER_PCT_MULTIPLIER`.
4. The underlying collateral token (`COLL`) must be supported and recognized by external protocols interacting with the auction.
5. Network conditions or gas prices allow timely calls to the strategy’s `_tend()` function to trigger auctions when collateral is available.

### Impact

The protocol and its users suffer an approximate loss proportional to the undervaluation caused by the precision loss in the auction starting price calculation. Specifically, collateral assets may be sold at a price lower than their true market value, resulting in economic loss. Attackers or opportunistic bidders gain from purchasing collateral at a discount due to the artificially low starting price.

This breaks the core invariant that auctions must start at or above a minimum buffered fair price to preserve collateral value and protect the protocol’s solvency. The precision loss causes the auction to start below this safe threshold, undermining the system’s economic soundness.

While the exact dollar amount depends on auction volume and collateral prices, even a small percentage underpricing can compound into significant losses over time. Therefore, the impact is financially material and risks protocol value leakage to attackers.

### Mitigation

To prevent precision loss, reorder the arithmetic operations in the auction starting price calculation by performing multiplication before division. For example, calculate as:

```solidity
AUCTION.setStartingPrice(
    (_available * _price * _bufferPercentage) / (WAD * WAD * WAD)
);
```

Alternatively, use a higher-precision math library or fixed-point arithmetic utilities that safely handle decimals and prevent intermediate truncation.

Adding thorough unit tests to cover edge cases in price calculations and integrating static analysis tools to detect improper operator precedence can help catch similar issues early.

Finally, consider setting a minimum acceptable starting price invariant that reverts if violated, ensuring the auction never begins below a safe price floor.
